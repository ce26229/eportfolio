About me

Chris Evans

I am an IT service management (ITSM) consultant with 30 years’ experience. I began in technical support, built a service management function for London Fire Brigade and went on to lead service architecture in defence. I now run FlowSM Ltd and advise on how government product teams manage live services.

I am studying for the MSc Information Technology Management at the University of Essex Online. I bring two perspectives to the programme. As a service desk manager, I was responsible for restoring the systems people relied on, including emergency dispatch. As a consultant, I design the operating models, information and governance that help teams take responsibility for those services.

Professional experience

Director and Principal ITSM Consultant · FlowSM Ltd

October 2025 to present

At FlowSM, I lead a service management transformation in a Grade 7-equivalent advisory role for a major government department. The work brings eight priority service management practices together across products and operational teams. The aim is to make services more consistent, clarify who owns them and give teams a reliable basis for governing their operation.

I design the underlying service management information model. This means deciding what teams need to record and how the records relate to one another so that the same information supports reporting, assurance, tooling and operational decisions. I work with product, delivery and operations colleagues to turn requirements into processes, data definitions, workflows and reporting arrangements they can use. Part of that work is agreeing who is accountable for a service and what evidence shows it is ready to operate.

Digital Service Management Lead · Fujitsu Services Ltd

April 2015 to October 2025

I led a 30-person service architecture function across Fujitsu Defence and the wider digital services portfolio. I recruited and developed the team, planned workloads and managed performance. We supported more than ten complex, multi-stage bids for multi-million-pound opportunities, as well as major service transitions and changes. I led the service management design for that work, including governance, costing, resourcing and operational readiness.

For new and changed services, I owned the design and transition through to acceptance and controlled handover into live operations. That included the processes, documentation and tooling that operational teams would inherit. I introduced reusable design packs, governance models and ITSM frameworks. These cut the effort required to produce bid material and supporting artefacts by 50%, improved consistency and reduced rework.

I also worked with stakeholders across national boundaries to shape service management strategy and align processes with business and delivery needs. Across bids, transitions and major changes, we drew on ITIL, the service management framework, and Service Integration and Management (SIAM), an approach to coordinating multiple providers.

Service Asset and Configuration Manager · Sopra Steria

March 2014 to April 2015

I was the ITIL subject matter expert on an account management team supporting a Ministry of Defence agency. I designed, implemented and managed service asset and configuration management (SACM) processes for more than 30,000 assets across multiple technologies, networks and security classifications.

By reconciling records more rigorously, improving data quality and tightening configuration controls, I raised the accuracy of configuration management database (CMDB) records and asset audits to 95%. Better control throughout the asset lifecycle also improved visibility and reduced write-offs. Repeatable evidence packs and dashboards helped cut audit preparation effort by 80%.

Technical Configuration Manager · ING / Barclays Direct

June 2012 to September 2013

As the ITIL expert on the process steering group, I supported service improvement programmes and ITSM transformation. I introduced a central licence repository and led the implementation of a software asset management system. This improved audit accuracy and cost control. The controls reduced the risk of licence non-compliance, and rationalising licences delivered annualised savings of 40%.

I also designed and implemented a new ITSM toolset. My remit covered the data structures and classification schemes, the screens staff used to enter records and the reports the organisation needed from those records.

Service Support Manager · London Fire Brigade

August 2002 to August 2011

I built a service management function from the ground up to support 7,500 staff across 113 sites. Our remit included emergency dispatch systems and covered on-site and remote support, from first-line enquiries through to third-line technical support. I designed the operating model, processes, tooling and team structure. I trained staff who came from outside IT to run a professional service desk that received the Service Desk Institute’s “Top Service Desk” award during Service Desk Week.

I managed around 50 people in support and process roles, with responsibility for recruitment, mentoring, training, performance and absence management. The team sustained first-contact resolution above 80% and customer satisfaction above 85%. Major incident management and structured reviews of underlying problems helped us restore services more effectively.

I introduced ITIL-based processes and supporting tools for incident, problem, change, asset and configuration management. The approach was still in live use more than 15 years later. I also brought the IT, Human Resources, Facilities and Fire Safety service desks together so staff had a single point of entry for all four services.

As the organisation’s IT customer relationship manager, I established the IT User Forum, managed suppliers and resolved service issues. I introduced reporting against key performance indicators (KPIs) and service level agreements (SLAs), and presented the results and proposed improvements to senior leaders and stakeholder forums.

Earlier career

I began as an AS/400 Systems Manager at IBM (1996–1998) before moving into support management as Break/Fix Helpdesk Manager at GE Capital IT Solutions (1998–2000). I then worked as Helpdesk Team Leader at Datalect Ltd (2000–2001) and Customer Support Manager at Qanat Software Ltd (2001–2002). This progression from systems management through technical support to responsibility for customers and teams formed the basis of my later service management work.

Areas of expertise

Strategy, transformation and governance. I design service operating models for defence, public sector and highly regulated environments. My work covers governance, assurance, continual improvement, executive stakeholder engagement and service reviews. I use KPIs, SLAs and operational level agreements (OLAs) to connect performance reporting with decisions about the service.

Service design, transition and supplier integration. I shape solutions and service architectures for bids, design support models and service desks, and prepare services for operational acceptance. This includes integrating suppliers through SIAM and managing third-party performance.

Service operations, information and tooling. My practice covers incident, problem, change, request, knowledge and service catalogue management. I also work on asset lifecycle control, CMDB governance and audit readiness. My tooling experience includes ServiceNow, Marval and Jira Service Management.

People and capability. I build and lead service teams, recruit and mentor staff, develop professional capability and manage performance. That experience spans both frontline support and specialist service architecture.

Qualifications and professional development

  • Service integration: Scopism SIAM Essentials (2026) and SIAM Foundation.
  • Agile and project management: Certified Agile Service Manager (CASM) and PRINCE2 Practitioner.
  • Cloud computing: AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner and Microsoft Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900).

Professional affiliations

I am a PeopleCert ITIL Ambassador and a member of the IT Service Management Forum (itSMF), the XLA Institute and the Value Stream Consortium.
